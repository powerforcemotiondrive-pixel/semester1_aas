GSEA Semester 1 Lesson Outline

Session 3: Building and Supporting Arguments

Essential professional and technical vocabulary covering operational concepts, analytical frameworks, and formal communication structures.

Toulmin Model

01

A six-element framework for structuring rigorous academic arguments: Claim, Data, Warrant, Backing, Qualifier, Rebuttal.

Hedging Device

02

Cautious language (it suggests, arguably, appears to indicate) used to calibrate certainty and avoid dogmatic overstatement.

Concession Marker

03

Discourse transitions (granted that, notwithstanding, while acknowledging) that concede a counter-point before refuting it.

Ad Hominem Fallacy

04

A flawed rhetorical tactic attacking an opponent's character rather than addressing the substance of their logical premise.

Straw Man Fallacy

05

Misrepresenting an opponent's argument into an exaggerated, easily defeated caricature to avoid genuine debate.

Empirical Evidence

06

Verifiable data derived from scientific observation, experimentation, or statistical sampling supporting an academic claim.

Carbon Border Tax (CBAM)

07

A tariff on carbon-intensive imports designed to prevent carbon leakage and level the playing field for clean domestic industry.

Mandatory Modal Shift

08

Statutory regulations compelling travelers or freight to use rail rather than road or air on competitive corridors.

Precautionary Principle

09

The doctrine that preventative action should be taken in the face of scientific uncertainty regarding environmental harm.

Substantive Rebuttal

10

A counter-argument backed by empirical data that invalidates the underlying premise of a competing claim.

Dialectical Synthesis

11

The resolution of opposing thesis and antithesis arguments into a cohesive, higher-order policy compromise.

Logical Fallacy

01

A flaw in reasoning that undermines the validity and soundness of an argumentative structure.

Slippery Slope

13

An unfounded assertion that an initial action will inevitably lead to an extreme, disastrous chain of consequences.

Burden of Proof

14

The procedural obligation of the party proposing a controversial claim or policy change to substantiate it with evidence.

Oxford Debate Format

15

A structured competitive debate protocol where teams argue for or against a specific motion under strict time limits.

Rhetorical Warrant

16

The underlying logical principle or assumption that connects supporting data to an overarching argumentative claim.

Nuanced Qualifier

17

Terms (predominantly, under specific constraints, in most verified instances) establishing limits on a claim's universality.

Peer-Reviewed Consensus

18

The collective judgment of international scientific experts verified through rigorous independent methodological review.
